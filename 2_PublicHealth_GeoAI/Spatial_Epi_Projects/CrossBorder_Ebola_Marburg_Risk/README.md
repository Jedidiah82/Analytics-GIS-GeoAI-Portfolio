# 🧳 Cross-Border Ebola & Marburg Risk Mapping

## Overview

This project focuses on geospatial risk modeling along Liberia’s borders with Guinea, Sierra Leone, and Côte d’Ivoire, supporting outbreak preparedness and cross-border surveillance planning.

The analysis integrates mobility corridors, ecological suitability factors, and vulnerability indicators to identify high-risk zones for disease transmission and prioritize surveillance efforts.

---

## Objectives

- Identify high-risk border regions for Ebola and Marburg transmission  
- Support cross-border surveillance and preparedness planning  
- Inform resource allocation and targeted intervention strategies  

---

## Methods

- Mobility corridor analysis using transportation and movement patterns  
- Ecological suitability modeling using environmental and geographic factors  
- Vulnerability scoring based on population and access indicators  
- GIS-based spatial analysis using ArcGIS and GeoPandas  

---

## Outputs

- Cross-border risk maps  
- Priority surveillance zones  
- Decision-support layers for outbreak preparedness

## Ebola Surveillance Data Management & Harmonization (Liberia)

During the West Africa Ebola outbreak, I contributed to national surveillance data systems by leading data cleaning, consolidation, and harmonization of Ebola datasets in Liberia.

- Cleaned and standardized multi-source surveillance and contact-tracing data  
- Consolidated fragmented datasets into consistent, analysis-ready formats  
- Ensured data quality, completeness, and consistency under outbreak conditions  
- Delivered validated datasets to the Ministry of Health and international partners (WHO, CDC)  

**Contribution to Outputs:**  
The processed datasets supported WHO and partner-produced epidemic mapping and reporting outputs used for real-time outbreak monitoring and response coordination.
